Two equations describe every wave, whether it is water, sound or an electromagnetic wave.

<div class="callout callout-formula"><p class="callout-title">Formula</p><p><strong>wave speed = frequency × wavelength</strong>   v = f × λ   (v in m/s, f in Hz, λ in m)<strong>frequency = 1 ÷ time period</strong>   f = 1 ÷ T   (T in s)</p></div>

![Diagram](./img/fig-03.png)

**Formula triangle for v = f × λ**

**Worked example.** A sound wave of frequency 512 Hz travels at 340 m/s. Wavelength λ = v ÷ f = 340 ÷ 512 = 0.66 m.

**Worked example.** A wave has a period of 0.040 s. Frequency f = 1 ÷ 0.040 = 25 Hz.

<div class="callout callout-tip"><p class="callout-title">Key facts</p><p>The wave speed depends on the <strong>material</strong>, not on the frequency.For the same speed, a higher frequency means a shorter wavelength.Convert ms to s and kHz/MHz to Hz before using the equations.</p></div>
