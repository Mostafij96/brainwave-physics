Forces explain why things start moving, stop moving or change direction. Isaac Newton summarised this in three laws.

## First law: inertia

> An object stays at rest, or keeps moving at a constant velocity, unless a **resultant force** acts on it.

That is why passengers lurch forward when a bus brakes suddenly. Their bodies want to keep moving.

## Second law: F = ma

> **F = m × a**

- `F` = resultant force (newtons, N)
- `m` = mass (kg)
- `a` = acceleration (m/s²)

**Example:** A 1200 kg car accelerates at 2.5 m/s². The resultant force is 1200 × 2.5 = **3000 N**.

Rearranged: a = F ÷ m. The same force gives a *smaller* acceleration to a *heavier* object.

## Third law: action and reaction

> If object A pushes on object B, then B pushes back on A with an **equal and opposite** force.

When you push a wall, the wall pushes back on you with the same size of force. The two forces act on **different objects**, so they do not cancel each other out.

## Weight and mass

| | Mass | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | Amount of matter | Force of gravity on the object |
| Unit | kg | N |
| Formula | | **W = m × g** (g ≈ 10 N/kg) |

## Exam tips

- Draw a **free-body diagram** and label every force before calculating.
- "Resultant force" means the *net* force after combining all forces.
- Never mix up mass (kg) and weight (N).
