### Charge and Current (Spec 2.14–2.16)

Electric current is the RATE OF FLOW OF CHARGE past a point in a circuit.

**Q = I × t**    →    charge (C) = current (A) × time (s)

- Spec 2.16: Electric current in solid metallic conductors is a flow of NEGATIVELY CHARGED FREE ELECTRONS — from the negative terminal to the positive terminal of the battery.
- Conventional current: By convention (historical agreement), we show current flowing from + to − (opposite to electron flow). Both are used and must be understood.

### Voltage — Energy per Unit Charge (Spec 2.20–2.21)

Voltage tells us how much energy is given to (or taken from) each coulomb of charge passing through a component.

**1 Volt  =  1 Joule per Coulomb**      E = Q × V      (energy = charge × voltage)

A 9V battery gives 9 joules of energy to every coulomb of charge that passes through it.

The voltmeter connected to a component measures the energy transferred per coulomb passing through that component.

### Series Circuit Calculations (Spec 2.19P)

- Rule 1 — Current: Current is the SAME throughout a series circuit: I₁ = I₂ = I\_total
- Rule 2 — Voltage: Voltages across components ADD UP to the supply: V₁ + V₂ = V\_supply
- Rule 3 — Resistance: Total resistance = sum of individual: R\_total = R₁ + R₂
- Method: Step 1: Find R\_total. Step 2: Find I = V\_supply ÷ R\_total. Step 3: Find V₁ = I×R₁ and V₂ = I×R₂. Step 4: Check V₁+V₂ = V\_supply ✓

| **❌ COMMON MISCONCEPTION** | **✅ THE TRUTH** |
| --- | --- |
| 'Current is used up as it flows around a series circuit' | Current is the SAME at every point in a series circuit. It is ENERGY (voltage) that is shared, not current. |
| 'Voltage across a parallel branch increases when more branches are added' | Voltage stays the SAME across parallel branches. Only the total current from the battery increases. |
| 'E = QV only applies to batteries' | E = QV applies to ANY component — it gives the energy transferred when charge Q passes through it. |
