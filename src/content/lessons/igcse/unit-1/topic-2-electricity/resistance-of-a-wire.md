### Factors Affecting Resistance

- Length (L): Longer wire → MORE resistance (more collisions for electrons). R ∝ L
- Cross-sectional area (A): Thicker wire → LESS resistance (more paths for electrons). R ∝ 1/A
- Material: Different materials have different resistivities (ρ). Copper &lt; Nichrome.
- Temperature: Higher temperature → MORE resistance (ions vibrate more, impede electron flow).

Formula:  R = ρL/A  where ρ = resistivity (Ω·m), L = length (m), A = cross-sectional area (m²)

**For IGCSE, the key result is: R is DIRECTLY PROPORTIONAL to L (graph = straight line through origin).**

### Practical Procedure

1. Set up: cell, variable resistor (to keep current low), ammeter in series, nichrome wire on a ruler, voltmeter across the wire section.

2. Move the second crocodile clip to lengths: 20, 40, 60, 80, 100 cm.

3. Record ammeter (I) and voltmeter (V) readings at each length.

4. Calculate R = V ÷ I for each length.

5. Plot R (y-axis) vs L (x-axis). Draw best-fit straight line.

6. Conclusion: straight line through origin → R ∝ L ✓

### Control Variables

Keep these the SAME throughout: (1) Cross-sectional area of the wire. (2) Material of the wire. (3) Temperature of the wire (take readings quickly or allow cooling).

| **❌ COMMON MISCONCEPTION** | **✅ THE TRUTH** |
| --- | --- |
| 'The ammeter goes across (in parallel with) the component' | WRONG — ammeter goes IN SERIES. Voltmeter goes across (in parallel with) the component. |
| 'I can use any wire for this experiment' | The wire must be NICHROME (or a specified material). Copper has very low resistance and would give tiny, inaccurate readings. |
