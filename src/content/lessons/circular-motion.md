An object moving in a circle at constant speed is still **accelerating**, because its direction is always changing. The force that causes this is called the **centripetal force**, and it always points toward the centre of the circle.

## Angular displacement and speed

Angles in A Level Physics are measured in **radians**.

> 2π rad = 360°

Angular speed (ω) is the angle swept out per second:

> **ω = Δθ ÷ t = 2π ÷ T = 2πf**

- `T` = period (time for one revolution, s)
- `f` = frequency (revolutions per second, Hz)

Linear speed and angular speed are linked by:

> **v = r ω**

## Centripetal acceleration

> **a = v² ÷ r = r ω²**

## Centripetal force

Using F = ma:

> **F = m v² ÷ r = m r ω²**

**Example:** A 0.50 kg ball on a string moves in a circle of radius 0.80 m at 4.0 m/s.

F = (0.50 × 4.0²) ÷ 0.80 = **10 N**

## Important points

- Centripetal force is **not a new type of force**. It is the name for whatever force provides the inward pull (tension, gravity, friction, normal reaction).
- The force is **perpendicular** to the velocity, so it changes direction but does **not** change speed and does no work.

## Exam tips

- Always convert degrees to radians and rpm to rad/s before using ω.
- State clearly **what provides** the centripetal force in the situation.
