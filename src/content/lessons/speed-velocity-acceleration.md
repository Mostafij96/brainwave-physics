Motion is the starting point of physics. Everything that moves, from a cyclist to a rocket, can be described using three ideas: **speed**, **velocity** and **acceleration**.

## Speed

Speed tells you how fast an object is moving. It does not care about direction.

> **speed = distance ÷ time**

Speed is measured in metres per second (`m/s`) or kilometres per hour (`km/h`).

**Example:** A car travels 150 m in 10 s. Its speed is 150 ÷ 10 = **15 m/s**.

## Velocity

Velocity is speed **in a given direction**. A car moving at 15 m/s north has a velocity of 15 m/s north.

| Quantity | Type | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Speed | Scalar (size only) | 15 m/s |
| Velocity | Vector (size + direction) | 15 m/s north |

## Acceleration

Acceleration is how quickly velocity changes.

> **a = (v − u) ÷ t**

- `a` = acceleration (m/s²)
- `v` = final velocity (m/s)
- `u` = initial velocity (m/s)
- `t` = time taken (s)

**Example:** A bike speeds up from 2 m/s to 8 m/s in 3 s.

a = (8 − 2) ÷ 3 = **2 m/s²**

## Exam tips

- Always write the **unit** in your final answer.
- If an object slows down, the acceleration is **negative** (deceleration).
- On a distance–time graph the **gradient is speed**. On a velocity–time graph the **gradient is acceleration** and the **area under the graph is distance**.

## Quick check

1. A runner covers 400 m in 50 s. What is the average speed?
2. A train goes from 10 m/s to 30 m/s in 8 s. What is its acceleration?

*Answers: 8 m/s, 2.5 m/s².*
